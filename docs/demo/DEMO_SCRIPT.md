## Segment 1: Model and Design: why we built it this way (Member 1, 2 min)
For each decision: the problem, our choice, why, and the alternative we rejected.

- **A full-stack web app (the brief's option C).** All three roles work at desks and need no install. One backend holds every rule, so no screen can skip a step, and each role gets its own interface, as the brief asks. We rejected a mobile app (a second codebase for staff who work on laptops) and a desktop app (installed and updated on every machine).
- **Java 17 + Spring Boot.** Java is where the course's OOP lives: interfaces, abstract classes, strong typing. Spring's dependency injection finds every strategy, observer and channel by itself, which is what makes our patterns pluggable. We rejected Python/FastAPI and Node because looser typing makes the design less explicit.
- **PostgreSQL.** Incident data is relational (reporter, technician, service, category, notes, audit) and needs reliable transactions, because an SLA breach must never be lost. We rejected MongoDB because the workload and service reports need joins.
- **One layered app with an event bus inside**, not microservices: the right size for three people. The bus gives us loose coupling without running a message broker.
- **Claude for the AI helpers, always with a rules fallback.** The brief rewards intelligent features and third-party services. Claude reads screenshots, chats, turns plain English into search filters and rewords updates. It only suggests and never saves anything on its own, every answer is checked against our own lists, and each helper has a rules version underneath. We rejected AI-only features: no key or no credits would mean no app.
- **The model:** the use case diagram (three actors), then the class diagram. Point to **inheritance** only where something truly is a kind of something else (Employee, Technician and Administrator are Users). Then **composition** (an Incident owns its SLA tracker, notes and resolution), **aggregation** (technicians and their skill categories) and the **interfaces** (IncidentState, PriorityStrategy, AssignmentStrategy, IncidentObserver, NotificationChannel). Also say **where we chose not to inherit**: categories are data, not subclasses, so an admin can add one without code.
- **Tools, named openly:** "The brief allows packages and AI tools. Inside the app we call Claude through Anthropic's API. While building it we used [say what you used, for example AI help for boilerplate and diagrams]. Every rule is pinned by an automated test, and we reviewed the code against the brief line by line."

## Recovery tips
- "No technician available": that technician is at capacity, so raise their maximum (the number after the slash under *Workload*) in *People*.
- A duplicate blocks a report you wanted: click *It's a different problem — submit anyway*.
- No duplicate warning in step 6 of Segment 2: INC-000001's title or description isn't exactly the script text. Restart, and check both fields before **Send report** next time.
- The dashboard's live marker stays on "Connecting…": reload the tab once.
- The escalation needs 2 full minutes with no change to the incident. *Check deadlines now* only runs the check at once; it can't skip the wait, so fill the time with step 3 of Segment 4.
- The AI takes more than about 10 seconds: fill in the fields by hand and carry on. The SLA clock doesn't wait. One slow or failed answer only affects that request. Two in a row put every AI feature in rules mode for 60 seconds (the indicator says *AI not responding*), and the bell updates in that minute stay templates tagged Rules; carry on, or switch **AI assistance** off and on in *Settings* to end the wait at once.
- The bell's newest update still says Rules: the AI wording arrives a few seconds after the change. Wait a moment or reopen the bell; after about 10 seconds, read the template and carry on.
- The chat's summary picks the wrong service or category: click **Edit**, choose Desk Phones (and *Let the system decide* for the category) in the form and press **Send report**. If the chat itself fails, close it (what Meera typed goes into the form) or fill in the form: title "Desk phone cannot dial", What happened "My desk phone cannot dial out since this morning", service Desk Phones, impact Low, urgency Medium, 1 person affected.
- The top bar says **Rules mode**: the reason is next to it. *No key*: check the `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=` line in `~/.config/i-itsm/ai.env`, then run `docker compose --env-file ~/.config/i-itsm/ai.env up -d` again (it restarts the backend with the key, so log in again in every tab). *Key not accepted*: put a working key in that file and run the same command. *AI not responding*: two calls in a row failed; the app tries again after 60 seconds, or at once if you switch **AI assistance** off and on. *Switched off*: turn **AI assistance** back on in *Settings*. *Today's limit reached* or *credits ran out*: record anyway in rules mode and follow the *Rules mode* notes in each step.
- Recorded too slowly and the P1 breached? Restart and record Segments 2 and 3 again: run `docker compose down -v && docker compose --env-file ~/.config/i-itsm/ai.env up -d` (about a minute; `-v` wipes the old incidents so the numbers start again at INC-000001). `-v` also resets the deadlines, so set the demo SLA for P1 again (Before recording, step 6). A restart also signs every tab out, so log in again in all four, and check the indicator says AI live. To keep logins across restarts, add `JWT_SECRET=` with 32+ characters to the same `~/.config/i-itsm/ai.env` file, or set it in your shell.
- Don't over-claim: there are no Playwright tests, "correlation" means similarity plus the same service, and the AI only suggests: it never saves a ticket, a category or a priority on its own.
